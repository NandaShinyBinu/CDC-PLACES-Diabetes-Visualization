# Geographic Patterns in Diabetes and Related Risk Factors Across U.S. Counties

## Project Overview

This project explores geographic patterns in diabetes prevalence across U.S. counties using the CDC PLACES 2025 dataset. Tableau was used to create interactive visualizations examining county-level diabetes prevalence and its relationship with selected health risk factors.

The goal is to identify geographic differences, highlight counties with higher estimated diabetes prevalence, and explore potential associations between diabetes and obesity.

## Objectives

- Examine geographic variation in diabetes prevalence across U.S. counties.
- Identify the 10 counties with the highest estimated diabetes prevalence.
- Explore the relationship between diabetes and obesity prevalence.
- Present public health data through clear and informative visualizations.

## Dataset

**Source:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), PLACES: Local Data for Better Health, 2025 release.

The dataset contains county-level estimates of diabetes prevalence and related health indicators.

Selected variables include:
- Diabetes prevalence
- Obesity prevalence
- Physical inactivity prevalence
- Smoking prevalence
- High blood pressure prevalence
- High cholesterol prevalence

## Tools and Technologies

- Tableau Public – Data visualization and dashboard development
- Microsoft Excel – Data inspection and preparation
- CSV – Dataset storage and handling

## Visualizations

### 1. County-Level Diabetes Prevalence Map

Displays the geographic distribution of estimated diabetes prevalence across U.S. counties.

### 2. Top 10 Counties by Diabetes Prevalence

A horizontal bar chart highlighting the 10 counties with the highest estimated diabetes prevalence.

### 3. Diabetes and Obesity Relationship

A scatter plot exploring the association between county-level diabetes and obesity prevalence, including a linear trend line.

### 4. Interactive Dashboard

Combines the three visualizations to provide an overview of geographic patterns and potential relationships between the selected health indicators.

## Key Findings

- Diabetes prevalence varies across U.S. counties.
- Several counties show comparatively high estimated diabetes prevalence.
- The scatter plot suggests a positive association between county-level obesity and diabetes prevalence.

These findings describe population-level patterns and do not establish causation.

## Public Health Relevance

County-level analysis can help public health professionals identify geographic areas that may benefit from additional diabetes prevention, screening, health education, and community-based interventions.

## Limitations

- CDC PLACES estimates are modeled population-level estimates, not individual-level diagnoses.
- County-level associations cannot establish cause-and-effect relationships.
- Findings may be affected by differences in local populations and estimation methods.
